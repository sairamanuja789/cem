**Overall assembly:** 120.0 × 27.0 × 120.0 mm. The pads set the outer envelope: the frame's 119 mm
plus the pads makes 120 mm, and the pads stand 0.7 mm proud on each face, giving 25 + 1.4 ≈ 27.0 mm
deep. The "133.1 × 27.0 × 133.1 mm" in the earlier inspection is most likely a non-optimal bounding
box (built from B-spline control points and tolerances). The optimal box agrees with the nominal
120 × 120 × 25 mm frame plus pads.

## 3. The invalid solid: the frame

- **Statuses:**
  - one face has **BRepCheck_InvalidImbricationOfWires** (status 23);
  - its shell reports **BadOrientationOfSubshape** (status 32) as a consequence.
  - No other face, edge or vertex errors.
- **The failing face (#420):**
  - a **conical** surface, area 3154 mm², spanning Y 0.30..11.15 and out to r ≈ 58 mm. This is the
    tapered inner wall on the stator side.
  - It has 7 wires: an outer wire of 44 edges, plus 6 small inner wires at r ≈ 55–56 mm, Y
    0.30..6.65. These are where 6 of the stator vanes meet the wall.
- **Diagnosis:**
  - the inner wires (vane-root cut-outs) are not properly nested inside the outer wire in the face's
    parameter space;
  - they probably touch or cross the outer boundary where the vane roots run out at the face's lower
    edge (Y = 0.30).
  - This is typical of a simplified or IP-modified model whose vanes were trimmed into the cone.
- **Automatic repair:** `ShapeFix_Face` and `ShapeFix_Shape` both leave the solid invalid (volume
  unchanged at 55 019 mm³).
- **Repair options** (only if ever needed for meshing):
  - rebuild the cone face with the vane roots fused into the wall, or
  - remove the 6 vane-root wires and re-fuse the vanes with a Boolean union.
  - Either is a manual defeaturing job.

## 5. Is it suitable for a preliminary CFD mesh?

**No. Do not use it for CFD.**

1. **The manufacturer's terms forbid it:** the README explicitly says not to use these models for
   performance simulations.
2. **It isn't representative:** the impeller and internal frame structures are modified, so any CFD
   result would describe a fictional fan. It couldn't validate our CFD (Gate 1) or calibrate
   anything.
3. **It isn't clean:** the frame solid is invalid and automatic repair fails.
   - The impeller is a valid closed solid with 33 faces and could technically be meshed.
   - The rotor–stator assembly would need manual defeaturing first (the conical vane-root face, the
     label, the pads).

## 7. Engineering note for the owner

The reference fan uses a **stator**, with 11 guide vanes downstream of the rotor. ADR-016 decided
**no stator in v1**. A stator turns the swirl the rotor leaves behind into static pressure, which is
a large part of how high-pressure 120 mm fans reach their static-pressure ratings. This is not a
reason to copy the Noctua stator. It is a reason to reconsider whether a v1 without a stator can
reach a realistic duty, which is an owner decision for ADR-016.

## 8. Recommendations

1. Keep the STEP outside the repository; record only its URL, the access date, the hash and this
   audit.
2. Use only the envelope and mounting data (with this source recorded) for integration checks of
   our rotor and duct.
3. Do not mesh, simulate or measure blade parameters from this model. Leave blade chord, pitch,
   airfoil, thickness, hub and tip clearance of the real fan **UNKNOWN**.
4. Gate 1 (CFD credibility) still needs a fan whose geometry we may simulate. In practice that means
   a physical fan we measure ourselves, or a published research geometry with test data.
5. Owner decision: should the stator question in ADR-016 be revisited?
